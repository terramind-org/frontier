# Fluid regularity claims: source audit (21 November 2026)

## Status at the cutoff

The Clay Mathematics Institute has **not announced a formal acceptance or prize award** for a Navier–Stokes solution. Its September 11 statement says the problem has “apparently been settled,” but also says the prize evaluation process is deliberately unhurried and that it will provide updates. The official problem page continues to publish the problem and Fefferman's statement. The careful status is therefore: major claims are public; Clay has acknowledged the announcement and is evaluating it; no Clay determination is documented in the official material reviewed for this cutoff. This does not mean the mathematical arguments are false.

Fefferman's actual alternatives matter. A and B ask global smooth existence for arbitrary smooth data with **zero force**, on respectively R³ and the three-torus. C and D ask for breakdown for some smooth data **and some smooth external force**, on R³ and the torus. The force must satisfy the stated rapid spatial and temporal decay in the whole-space case, or be spatially periodic and uniformly rapidly decaying in time on the torus. “Forced” is not automatically outside the Clay problem: it is explicitly allowed in C/D. Euler sets viscosity to zero and is not itself one of the prize alternatives.

## Claims and what their statements cover

| Alpöge–Buckmaster, 20 Nov 2026, *Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations* and accompanying repository | **Unforced, viscous 3D Navier–Stokes**, with conclusions on R³ and T³, as summarized by the authors' Lean README/NOTICE. | Smooth compactly supported divergence-free data on R³ (and smooth periodic data on T³); claimed failure of any global smooth solution in the stated bounded-energy/periodicity classes, via finite-time singularity of a constructed solution. The authors' repository says its Lean package proves implications from a certificate but **contains no certificate data**; a hash is given in the paper and a separate rigorous interval-arithmetic check is required. | Buckmaster–Alpöge public statement, Sep 8 2026 | Forced blow-up for 3D incompressible porous media, Boussinesq and Euler; also mentions **hypo-dissipative** Navier–Stokes. | The statement says the first three use smooth forcing; it says the hypo-dissipative NS paper was not released because Lean verification had not finished and no presentable writeup existed. | Distinguish these from standard Navier–Stokes with Laplacian viscosity. The released forced Euler result then was not an unforced NS result. The Nov unforced Euler and NS papers are later and distinct claims. |

The Euler paper's singular quantity is vorticity, while its velocity stays bounded; this is consistent with Euler's inviscid equation and should not be paraphrased as velocity blow-up for Navier–Stokes. The OpenAI and November NS claims concern unbounded velocity in the presence of positive viscosity. Also distinguish classical smooth solutions up to a finite terminal time from statements about nonuniqueness of global weak/Leray–Hopf solutions: those are different regularity and uniqueness questions. The NS authors' Lean notice explicitly describes the conclusion as nonexistence of a global smooth bounded-energy solution for selected data; it is not a claim that every weak solution ceases to exist.

## What a checker does and does not settle

The NS repository's own README describes a layered proof: a certificate encodes finite data for an approximate self-similar profile; the formal library proves certificate ⇒ profile/breakdown implications; a separate rigorous interval-arithmetic computation is supposed to check the actual certificate. The public repository says that actual data and computation are absent, and that its patched package was not rebuilt when assembled. This is a concrete and useful verification checklist: (1) freeze the exact theorem statement and hypotheses; (2) make the finite certificate public and hash-bound; (3) independently reproduce the interval computation; (4) rebuild the exact source revision in a clean environment; (5) audit bridges from formalized statements to the paper and from the paper to Clay's alternatives. Proof checking of an implication is not checking an omitted premise.

OpenAI's September claim has a different proof shape: the paper states a constructed smooth-force counterexample and the repository links a Lean formalization. It should still be compared line-by-line with C/D's force, domain, energy, and solution requirements; the OpenAI announcement itself says it does not intend to claim the prize, while Clay's official process is distinct from a publisher's assertion.

## Relevance to the BSD attempt

No Navier–Stokes result implies anything about BSD. The transferable part is methodological only: state the target theorem exactly; keep assumptions explicit; formalize reusable intermediate implications; bind computational evidence to immutable inputs; independently reproduce numerical checks; and separately review whether the formal statement matches the intended mathematical claim. For a BSD attempt, this suggests a small, precisely scoped Lean lemma with all hypotheses visible and no hidden imported assumptions, followed by exact checker output and a human-readable explanation of the statement-to-problem match. It supplies no BSD conjecture, proof strategy, or evidence.
