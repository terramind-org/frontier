# Riemann hypothesis claims audit (snapshot: 21 November 2026)

## Status at Clay

Clay's [Unsolved archive](https://www.claymath.org/problem/unsolved/) still lists the Riemann Hypothesis under “Unsolved” as checked on 21 November 2026. That is the official status; a posted claim does not itself change it. Clay's [Millennium Prize rules](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/) say CMI considers a proposed solution only after publication in a qualifying outlet, at least two years have elapsed, and the solution has received general acceptance in the global mathematics community. The 20 November announcement below does not meet those stated criteria on this snapshot.

## Claims and results, distinguished by what their papers prove

### Levent Alpöge with Claude: claimed RH and Dirichlet GRH proof (20 November 2026)

[Alpöge's four-page note, “Remark”](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) says “I believe I have … discovered a proof” of RH, says the method extends to GRH and that the arguments have been formalized without assumptions, and links fourteen Lean archive parts. It describes an extremely ambitious route involving a Stepanov-style construction, iterated theta lifts, automorphic forms, and a slope inequality. The note itself says the formalization is enormous, that Alpöge had not yet mastered the surrounding mathematics, and that a readable exposition and explanation are future work. This is therefore a **full-proof claim**, not a zero-density estimate or a finite computation, but its mathematical content is not yet independently understood or accepted. The Lean files are primary evidence of a formalization claim, not by themselves evidence that its theorem statement is the intended RH/GRH statement or that the proof dependencies are sound; that requires independent inspection of the statement, imports/axioms, and kernel checking. Terence Tao's [contemporaneous note](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/unforced-euler-singularity-unforced-navier-stokes-singularity-and-the-generalized-riemann-hypothesis/) calls it an enormous Lean-only proof, says initial analysis suggests heavy use of Langlands and arithmetic geometry, and expects digestion to take time. It adds no independent verification.

**Hypotheses/scope:** author's stated claim is RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions; the note says ERH may follow with more time, which is prospective rather than a result. It does not claim GRH for elliptic-curve L-functions in the note. **Transfer:** if its claimed theorem really proves the standard Dirichlet GRH, that would not establish RH for elliptic-curve L-functions: those are degree-two automorphic L-functions, a different family. The author's suggested future extension is not a proof. The proposed automorphic/theta machinery might be relevant in principle, but transfer would require a theorem for the elliptic-curve L-function family and all needed hypotheses, not analogy.

### Anthropic/Alpöge–Furman: positive proportion on the critical line (10–21 August 2026)

The original paper is [Alpöge and Furman, “More than two thirds of the zeta zeros are simple and on the critical line,” arXiv:2608.13637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637), first posted 13 August and dated 21 August in its paper text. It proves unconditionally that at least (2/3) of nontrivial zeta zeros, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on ℜ(s)=1/2; with the Montgomery–Taylor window it gives a liminf of 0.67250… for simple critical-line zeros and 0.83625… distinct zeros. The paper says the argument was discovered and written by Claude and that its human authors verified it and take responsibility. It reports Lean 4 formalization. Youness Lamzouri's independent, shorter [arXiv:2609.02882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882), submitted 2 September (paper dated 10 September), proves the 67.25% / 83.62% bounds using a Hilbert-space inequality and unconditional pair-correlation input. These are **critical-line proportion results**, not RH: an unbounded remaining proportion of zeros may still lie off the line.

**Inputs/hypotheses:** the Alpöge–Furman abstract identifies the unconditional pair-correlation inputs of Aryan and Baluyot–Goldston–Suriajaya–Turnage-Butterbaugh, and says the result extends to primitive Dirichlet L-functions. The result is asymptotic in height; it is not conditional on RH. Lamzouri's formulation explicitly uses the unconditional pair-correlation theorem. **Transfer:** the authors expressly extend to primitive Dirichlet L-functions. Neither paper's stated theorem extends this result to elliptic-curve L-functions. Analogous pair-correlation or explicit-formula methods for degree-two automorphic L-functions would need their own analytic inputs and proof.

### OpenAI: “Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis” (30 September / 5 October 2026)

The [30 September paper](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026) claims a zero-free half-plane ℜ(s)>7/8 for zeta. The [5 October alternate paper](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-October-5-2026) proves the analogous fixed half-plane ℜ(s)>11/12 for every Dirichlet L-function and every finite-order Hecke L-function over ℚ(√−3), including zeta; it says this rules out Landau–Siegel zeros. This is a **zero-free-region result**. It excludes zeros near 1, but leaves the strip 1/2<ℜ(s)≤11/12 (or, for the 7/8 claim, 1/2<ℜ(s)≤7/8) unaddressed, so it is not RH/GRH. The October paper defines its quasi-RH conclusion as a fixed-width zero-free half-plane and makes no RH claim. These are preprints from OpenAI's math repository, not CMI determinations.

**Scope/conditions:** the October theorem is stated for all finite-order Hecke characters over the specified imaginary quadratic field and hence all Dirichlet characters, uniformly in conductor and height. The stated theorem is unconditional as written. **Transfer:** it does not state a zero-free result for elliptic-curve L-functions (degree two); the Dirichlet/finite-order Hecke family does not include those L-functions. Methods may inspire analogous results but no transfer follows.

### Guth–Maynard zero-density improvement (31 May 2024; revised 7 April 2026)

The original [Guth–Maynard paper, “New large value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials,” arXiv:2405.20552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20552), derives a zeta zero-density estimate (N(\sigma,T)\le T^{30(1-\sigma)/13+o(1)}) and prime asymptotics in intervals of length (x^{17/30+o(1)}). Zero density bounds how many zeros lie to the right of a given σ; it does not assert that this number is zero. The paper's statements are unconditional, with the stated asymptotics as (T\to\infty). This is progress toward RH, not a proof. **Transfer:** it is built around large values of Dirichlet polynomials and zeta zeros. Related techniques can be studied for other L-functions, but this theorem does not apply to elliptic-curve L-functions. A separate degree-two estimate with its own analytic hypotheses would be required.

### Computation: verified finite height, not all zeros

The rigorously checkable primary reference [Platt–Trudgian, “The Riemann hypothesis is true up to (3\cdot10^{12}),” arXiv:2004.09765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09765), posted 21 April 2020, uses interval/ball arithmetic and a variation of Turing's method to verify all nontrivial zeta zeros through height (3{,}000{,}175{,}332{,}800). It is **finite computational verification**: it cannot establish a statement about every height. The authors distinguish their rigorous verification from earlier large computations whose error control was less fully documented. There are later unverified/less rigorous computational announcements, but none changes the logical boundary: every finite-height check leaves infinitely many zeros unchecked. Methods for computations of elliptic-curve L-functions are possible, but finite verification likewise cannot prove their global RH.

## Takeaway for the research/formal attempt

As of 21 November 2026, classify the situation as: (i) Clay still lists RH unsolved; (ii) a newly announced, sweeping formal proof claim is under expert digestion; (iii) established 2026 advances prove positive proportions of critical-line zeros and fixed zero-free regions; (iv) zero-density and finite-height computation remain partial results. Do not use “two-thirds solved”: the proportion is a density lower bound, not a fraction of the conjecture proved. No checked result here transfers automatically to elliptic-curve L-functions. For a checker-tested attempt, treat the Alpöge Lean archive as a research lead only after checking the exact formal target and dependencies; for a modest reusable direction, inspect explicit-formula/pair-correlation techniques and identify which hypotheses are available for the target L-function. The repo's attempt workflow still requires choosing an actual catalog problem and following its checker and submission rules.
