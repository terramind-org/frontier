# Iwasawa approaches to the p-primary BSD formula

Research checked 21 November 2026. The results below concern elliptic curves over Q unless stated otherwise. “The p-part of BSD” means the equality of p-adic valuations in the leading-term formula, including the p-primary Tate–Shafarevich group and Tamagawa factors; it is not the full rational BSD conjecture unless every prime is covered and the analytic/algebraic rank equality is established.

## The mechanism

For a prime p, the cyclotomic Iwasawa algebra is typically Λ = Z_p[[Γ]], Γ = Gal(Q∞/Q). The algebraic object is the Pontryagin dual X of a Selmer group over Q∞; the analytic object is a p-adic L-function L_p(E) interpolating suitably normalized complex L-values. The main conjecture identifies their characteristic ideals, schematically char_Λ(X) = (L_p(E)), with local conditions and periods built into the precise formulation. Specializing at the trivial character, and combining interpolation with control theorems, extracts p-adic valuations of special values and Selmer groups. At analytic rank 1, the anticyclotomic direction over an imaginary quadratic field and a p-adic Gross–Zagier formula connect the derivative to Heegner points/regulators. Extra hypotheses are essential: a one-variable characteristic-ideal identity does not itself assert the full BSD leading-term formula in arbitrary rank.

Euler systems are the bridge from analytic classes to Selmer bounds. Kato constructs zeta cohomology classes from modular-curve K-theory/Beilinson elements, proves norm relations, and uses an explicit reciprocity law to identify their localizations with p-adic L-values. Euler-system descent gives one inclusion (analytic element divides/controls the Selmer characteristic ideal, with integrality and lattice qualifications). The reverse inclusion generally needs additional input: Eisenstein congruences and deformation theory (Skinner–Urban/Wan), or Heegner/Beilinson–Flach classes and anticyclotomic methods.

## Core primary sources and precise scope

* Kazuya Kato, “p-adic Hodge theory and values of zeta functions of modular forms,” *Astérisque* 295 (2004), 117–290. Stable source: https://www.numdam.org/article/AST_2004__295__117_0.pdf . Chapter III, especially Theorem 14.2 and Theorem 14.5, gives Euler-system Selmer bounds/Tamagawa consequences; Chapter IV, Theorem 17.4, proves torsionness of the ordinary Selmer dual and a divisibility toward the main conjecture (the statement includes the lattice/image hypotheses and an index factor); Theorem 18.4 gives a p-adic rank/corank inequality. The abstract explicitly says the p-adic zeta function vanishes to order at least rank E(Q). This is not equality of orders, nor arbitrary-rank strong BSD. Kato’s result supplies the “one-sided” theorem, not an unconditional full main conjecture for every E,p.
* Christopher Skinner and Eric Urban, “The Iwasawa Main Conjectures for GL₂,” *Invent. Math.* 195 (2014), 1–277. Author PDF: https://www.math.columbia.edu/~urban/eurp/MC.pdf . The paper’s Theorem 3.6.1 is a Hida-family/imaginary-quadratic divisibility theorem; Theorems 3.6.9 and 3.6.11 give ordinary elliptic-curve specializations. In particular, Theorem 3.6.11 assumes good ordinary reduction, irreducibility of E[p], and a prime q ≠ p with q || N where the residual representation is ramified; its rank-zero clause also assumes L(E,1) ≠ 0 and residual surjectivity. It yields the p-primary BSD equality in that rank-zero setting; if L(E,1)=0 it gives positive Selmer corank. These are substantial but not universal hypotheses.
* Robert Pollack and Tom Weston, “On the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves at supersingular primes,” *J. reine angew. Math.* 639 (2010), 1–37; and Shinichi Kobayashi, “Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves at supersingular primes,” *Invent. Math.* 152 (2003), 1–36. These introduce signed ± p-adic L-functions/Selmer groups (in the a_p=0 setup) to restore an integral main-conjecture formulation where the classical ordinary objects are not cotorsion/integral.
* Antonio Lei, David Loeffler, Sarah Livia Zerbes, “Wach modules and Iwasawa theory for modular forms,” *Asian J. Math.* 14 (2010), and their subsequent signed Coleman-map work develop the broader signed framework. Avoid conflating the classical ± statement with a theorem for every supersingular prime and arbitrary a_p.
* Kâzım Büyükboduk and Antonio Lei, “Iwasawa theory for elliptic curves over Q_∞,” (reference varies by formulation); for a clean supersingular primary result see Antonio Lei, “Iwasawa theory for modular forms at supersingular primes,” *Compos. Math.* 146 (2010), and the source below.
* Chan-Ho Kim, “Iwasawa Main Conjecture for Supersingular Elliptic Curves and BSD conjecture,” arXiv:1411.6352, first posted 20 Nov 2014, https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6352 . The paper proves Kobayashi’s ± main conjecture for good supersingular p with a_p=0 (odd p), reducing to a Greenberg main conjecture and using Beilinson–Flach explicit reciprocity. Its BSD corollaries are analytic rank 0 or 1, not arbitrary rank; see the paper’s main theorem and BSD corollary (Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3 in the arXiv version). Explicit infinite families in its appendix are examples, not a uniform theorem for all curves.
* Ashay Burungale, Francesc Castella, Christopher Skinner, “Base change and Iwasawa Main Conjectures for GL₂,” arXiv:2405.00270, first posted 1 May 2024; revised version dated 22 March 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00270 . Theorem 1.1.2 proves the cyclotomic ordinary main conjecture for p>3 under (irr_Q), E[p] irreducible, and a large-image condition (im); without (im), it gives the equality after tensoring with Q_p. Remark 1.1.3 notes (im) for non-CM curves for all sufficiently large p via Serre open image. Their anticyclotomic theorems (Theorems 1.2.2–1.2.3) require an imaginary quadratic K with the stated splitting/representation hypotheses. Corollary 1.3.1 deduces the p-part BSD formula only when analytic rank r is 0 or 1, under the corresponding hypotheses. Importantly, this removes the earlier auxiliary residual ramification condition in this setting, but still does not cover arbitrary E and arbitrary p.
* Ashay Burungale, Francesc Castella, Giada Grossi, Christopher Skinner, “Non-vanishing of Kolyvagin systems and Iwasawa theory,” arXiv:2312.09301, first posted 14 Dec 2023: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09301 . Theorem A proves cases of Kolyvagin’s nonvanishing conjecture for ordinary p split in K conditional on the relevant anticyclotomic main conjecture; the paper also treats a Tamagawa-factor refinement and analogous cyclotomic Beilinson–Kato systems. This strengthens the arithmetic bridge, but is not a proof of full strong BSD in all ranks.
* Florian Ito Sprung, “On Iwasawa main conjectures for elliptic curves at supersingular primes: Beyond the case a_p=0,” *Advances in Mathematics* 449 (July 2024), article 109741, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2024.109741 . It studies three supersingular main-conjecture formulations and reduces the chromatic main conjecture to a conjectured existence/reciprocity package for Beilinson–Flach classes; the abstract’s BSD consequence is the 3-part of the leading-term formula for analytic rank 0 or 1. Read the stated conjectural input carefully: this is not a blanket unconditional theorem for all supersingular p or all curves.
* David Burns, Masato Kurihara, Takamichi Sano, “On derivatives of Kato’s Euler system for elliptic curves,” *J. Math. Soc. Japan* 76(3) (July 2024), 855–919; DOI https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/90699069 ; author PDF https://kurihara.math.keio.ac.jp/bks4.pdf . The paper formulates the Generalized Perrin–Riou Conjecture. Its order-of-vanishing component is established in arbitrary rank under mild technical hypotheses, and Theorem 6.2 proves a higher-rank Rubin derivative formula; the authors explicitly describe a connection of the p-part BSD formula with the Iwasawa main conjecture in arbitrary rank and arbitrary reduction at p. Do not turn “connection” into “BSD proved”: the precise leading-term congruence/conjectural input is still needed, and the stronger Generalized Perrin–Riou congruence is not proved in general. Their rank-one specialization recovers Perrin–Riou’s expected logarithmic formula.

## What recent work does and does not establish

The strongest recent ordinary result located here is Burungale–Castella–Skinner (2024 preprint, 2026 revision): broad cyclotomic and anticyclotomic main conjectures for good ordinary primes under residual irreducibility and large-image/integrality conditions, followed by the p-part of BSD for analytic ranks at most one. That is an advance beyond the Skinner–Urban ramification hypothesis, not a result for every elliptic curve/prime. The 2024 Burns–Kurihara–Sano paper provides arbitrary-rank derivative/vanishing structure, including arbitrary reduction at p, but leaves a conjectural generalized reciprocity/leading-term layer; it should be cited as framework and partial theorem, not as arbitrary-rank strong BSD. Supersingular work has moved beyond a_p=0, but the 2024 Sprung paper explicitly uses a conjectural Beilinson–Flach package for its central chromatic main conjecture; its stated BSD output is limited to p=3 and ranks zero/one.

Accordingly, no cited result proves strong BSD for an arbitrary E/Q in arbitrary analytic rank, at every prime, without hypotheses. Remaining obstacles include: analytic rank equals Mordell–Weil rank and finiteness of Sha in general; the reverse main-conjecture divisibility outside known residual-image/local hypotheses; p=2 and exceptional primes; nonordinary/additive reduction formulations and integral control; higher-rank leading terms/regulators and nondegenerate p-adic heights; and combining prime-by-prime p-primary equalities into the exact integral BSD leading term. Any computational or formal attempt in this repository should target a sharply stated lemma or a finite explicit instance, and should not represent an Iwasawa implication as a proof of full BSD.

## Note for a checker-tested attempt

The most realistic formal target is an elementary formal consequence in a restricted, explicitly specified setting, or a machine-checkable verification of a finite arithmetic example. The cited Iwasawa theorems rely on deep constructions (Galois representations, Selmer complexes, Euler systems, reciprocity laws, p-adic L-functions) absent from a basic Lean library; formalizing their full hypotheses/conclusions is not a plausible short attempt. A proof-tier statement in the repository must be checked against its exact imported Lean definition, not a nearby conjectural BSD slogan. Do not select a prose problem unless explicitly requested, per repository policy.
