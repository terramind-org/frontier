# Independent review: finite descent certificate

## Verdict

The finite-level detection criterion and the conditional rational-solubility implication in `transverse-coset.md` are valid under its stated hypotheses. They do not provide an arithmetic existence result for a rational point, and therefore do not complete the weak BSD lower bound. I corrected one overstatement in the height discussion in the source note.

## Finite-level detection

Let `G=E(Q)`, `T=G_tors`, and `M=G/T`. Since `P` is non-torsion and primitive in the free lattice `M`, the saturated subgroup of `G` generated by `P` and torsion is `H=ZP+T`; its quotient by torsion is a direct summand of `M`. The finite-level subgroup is properly defined as the image of `H` in `G/p^nG` under the Kummer injection. The quotient `M/(Z[P])` is torsion-free, so membership at a finite level is exactly vanishing of all transverse free coordinates modulo `p^n`, with the full `p`-primary torsion contribution included. Prime-to-`p` torsion maps to zero in `G/p^nG`.

If a compatible inverse-limit class belongs to these images at an unbounded sequence of levels, then at every fixed level it belongs to the image: reduce from any sufficiently large selected level using compatibility. The preimages in the finite groups `H/p^mH` form compatible nonempty finite-level conditions; equivalently, compactness of the inverse limit gives a subsequential limit. This proves membership in the completed known subgroup. Thus for a class outside that subgroup, exclusion holds at all sufficiently large levels, not merely at one selected level. This step requires that the class really is in the inverse limit of the **classical** Selmer groups and that its representatives are compatible. A strict subspace alone supplies neither fact.

Torsion is handled correctly by including `T` in `H`; ignoring it could misclassify torsion translates. The primitive hypothesis is what makes the known free line a direct summand. Without it, the source correctly defines the saturated line `Q[P] ∩ M`; one must use that line in the finite-level comparison.

## Rational-solubility certificate

For each finite `n`, the Kummer exact sequence identifies the quotient of the classical Selmer group by `G/p^nG` with `Sha(E/Q)[p^n]`. The torsor attached to a Selmer element has a rational point exactly when its class in `H^1(Q,E)` is zero, equivalently its image in this Sha group is zero. Exactness then gives a rational point `Q_n ∈ G` whose Kummer image is the selected class. If the class is outside the finite-level image of `H`, `Q_n` cannot belong to `H`; therefore its free Mordell–Weil class is outside the rational span of `[P]`. This proves rank at least two.

This is a conditional certificate: it assumes rational solubility (or an equivalent proof of zero Sha image). Local solubility is already built into the Selmer condition and is insufficient. Finite-level exclusion detects transversality only. No argument in the source forces the zero Sha image at any level.

## Height and saturation caveats

The centered-coordinate construction is valid for a vector in `M ⊗ Z_p`, after choosing a full integral basis and lifts to `G`. The positive-definite canonical-height quadratic form gives the stated bound `||A|| r p^(2n)/4`. It depends on the chosen basis and is a worst-case bound for vectors in the Mordell–Weil completion. It cannot be applied to an arbitrary compatible Selmer class with a nonzero `T_p Sha` component, and it does not produce a rational point on a torsor.

The original source incorrectly called this scale sharp for every nonzero fixed coordinate. An ordinary integer coordinate such as `u=1` has centered representatives equal to 1 at all sufficiently large levels. The source is corrected to state only that `O(p^(2n))` is a uniform worst-case estimate; no uniform depth-independent bound over all completion vectors is supplied.

## Remaining unproved arithmetic input

The precise missing implication remains: for the specified transverse compatible class, prove that some finite-level component has zero image in `Sha(E/Q)[p^n]` (and hence is Kummer/rationally soluble). Neither compatibility, saturation, height scaling, finite-level exclusion, nor vanishing against a set of Cassels–Tate tests supplies this. The divisible radical and the absence of a proved exhaustive nondegeneracy theorem leave this as an arithmetic existence problem. The task establishes no such existence theorem and no unconditional weak BSD conclusion.
