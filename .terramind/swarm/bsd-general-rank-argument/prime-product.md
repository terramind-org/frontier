# Prime-product criterion: implications and obstruction

## Result

For E/Q, put N_p=#E^ns(F_p) for the nonsingular points on a minimal model and P_E(x)=product_{p<=x} N_p/p. At good p, N_p=p+1-a_p, so N_p/p is the reciprocal local Euler factor of L(E,s) at s=1; the finitely many bad-prime conventions change only a fixed nonzero constant.

Goldfeld's theorem is stated by Conrad for the reciprocal partial Euler product

    Q_E(x)=product_{p<=x} p/N_p = 1/P_E(x).

If `Q_E(x) ~ C_- (log x)^(-r)` for some `C_->0` and real `r`, then `L(E,s)` has no zeros for `Re(s)>1`, `ord_{s=1} L(E,s)=r`, and

    C_- = (L^(r)(E,1)/r!) / (sqrt(2) exp(r gamma)).

Equivalently, for the direct product `P_E` used here,

    P_E(x) ~ C_+ (log x)^r,  C_+ = r! sqrt(2) exp(r gamma) / L^(r)(E,1).

The numerator in `C_-` is the leading Taylor coefficient `L^(r)(E,1)/r!`; `L^(r)(E,1)` alone is the derivative. The finite bad-prime factors are included: for bad `p`, `N_p=p-a_p` and `N_p/p=1-a_p/p`, the reciprocal of the bad local Euler factor `1/(1-a_p p^-s)` at `s=1`. Conrad notes that his nonsingular-point convention at bad primes differs from the original BSD convention only by finite factors, which affect the constant but not the exponent. The full sharp-cutoff asymptotic is the hypothesis; Goldfeld does not derive it from Mordell-Weil points. In fact Conrad's Theorem 1.3 identifies that asymptotic with `psi_E(x)=o(x log x)`, stronger than the RH-scale estimate `O(x(log x)^2)`.

The available converse is weaker: Sheth proves that RH for L(E,s), together with analytic order r, yields the product asymptotic only for x outside a set of finite logarithmic measure (measure dx/x). It is not an unconditional all-cutoff result. Consequently, proving the original product asymptotic from rational points would imply the modern rank equality by Goldfeld; this route does not avoid the central hard step.

## What reductions of a basis say

Choose a Mordell-Weil basis P_1,...,P_r modulo torsion. At good p their reductions generate a subgroup H_p of E(F_p), so #H_p divides #E(F_p) and [E(F_p):H_p]=#E(F_p)/#H_p. This is divisibility only. Independence over Q gives no uniform lower bound for #H_p relative to p: reductions can become dependent or lie in a small subgroup. The finite group E(F_p) has at most two cyclic factors, and abstractly a prescribed small subgroup does not bound the order of its ambient group.

Even control of the index would not by itself recover the product asymptotic: `log P_E(x)=sum_{p<=x} log(N_p/p)` depends on the full Frobenius Euler-factor contribution, which is not encoded by the subgroup generated by the fixed reductions. A proposed arithmetic proof needs control at the full constant-term precision, establishing `sum_{p<=x} log(N_p/p)=r log log x+c_E+o(1)` for some finite constant `c_E`. An error merely `o(log log x)` can determine the exponent but does not imply Goldfeld's positive-constant asymptotic. Any decomposition into Frobenius contributions and subgroup/index terms must have combined error `o(1)` after the main term and constant are accounted for. Neither this precision nor even the leading coefficient follows from basis independence.

## Kummer/Chebotarev uniformity gap

For a fixed modulus m, Kummer theory and Chebotarev can describe primes where reductions of chosen independent points satisfy divisibility conditions modulo m, after accounting for the division/Kummer extension and its Galois image. Such fixed-extension densities do not supply estimates uniform as m grows with p or the product cutoff x. An index comparable to p requires divisibility information across many growing auxiliary moduli and, for the sharp product asymptotic, combined cumulative errors `o(1)` after the main term and constant are accounted for. Standard Chebotarev error terms depend on extension degree and discriminant; applying the theorem separately at each fixed modulus does not give the required uniform estimate.

Freiberg-Kurlberg is relevant but insufficient: under GRH for Dedekind zeta functions of the division fields (unconditionally in the CM case), they show the exponent e_p of E(F_p) divided by #E(F_p) has a positive average constant. This concerns exponent versus full group order, not the subgroup generated by a fixed Mordell-Weil basis, and does not estimate sum log(N_p/p) to the precision needed for the prime product. Its hypotheses themselves illustrate the strength of uniform division-field information.

## Deductions and precise missing lemma

1. If one proves for every E/Q that P_E(x)~C_E(log x)^{rank E(Q)} with C_E>0 for all x tending to infinity, Goldfeld gives weak BSD immediately. This premise is the original BSD product conjecture in its sharp-cutoff form.
2. Smoothing is not automatically interchangeable with the sharp prime cutoff. A smoothed logarithmic sum needs Tauberian control of prime-scale fluctuations to recover this exact asymptotic. No such control is supplied by basis reductions. Sheth's conditional converse having an exceptional set of finite logarithmic measure demonstrates the issue.
3. The missing lemma for the proposed point-based derivation is a uniform theorem controlling the mean logarithmic index of the reductions subgroup, together with compatible Frobenius-trace averaging, strongly enough to establish

       sum_{p<=x} log(N_p/p) = rank(E(Q)) log log x + c_E + o(1)

for some constant `c_E`. No source checked here proves this. The weaker `o(log log x)` error would not suffice for Goldfeld's theorem. If established, the displayed estimate gives `P_E(x)~exp(c_E)(log x)^rank`, and Goldfeld would then give weak BSD. This uniform estimate is the substantive unresolved step, not a formal consequence of reduction.

## Scope

This audits the prime-product route only. No unconditional product asymptotic or weak BSD theorem is established. The obstruction is the missing uniform control of full finite-group orders and Frobenius traces from reductions of a fixed Mordell-Weil basis at the precision needed for the sharp prime product.
