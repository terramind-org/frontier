# A rational point witnesses nonzero finite localization

## Claim and scope

Let (E/mathbf Q) have good ordinary reduction at an odd prime (p), and use the (p)-adic representation (V=V_pE). If (P\in E(mathbf Q)) has infinite order, then its global Kummer class in
\[
H^1_f(\mathbf Q,V)
\]
has nonzero restriction to (H^1_f(\mathbf Q_p,V)). This is an independent witness for the nonzero-localization hypothesis in Castella's revised §5.2; it does not assume a rank parity statement.

Assume in addition all hypotheses of Castella's revised §5.2/Choice 5.2.1, including (h1)–(h3), the auxiliary choices and nonzero twist values, and that
\(\dim_{\mathbf Q_p}\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbf Q,V)=2\). If there is such a rational point, then
\[
\operatorname{loc}_p\ne0,\qquad \kappa_p(E)\ne0,
\qquad \operatorname{Sel}_0(\mathbf Q,V)=\mathbf Q_p\,\kappa_p(E).
\]
Here the *strict class* \(\kappa_p(E)\) is nonzero and lies in the kernel of localization. It is not the class whose localization is nonzero: the point witnesses that the localization map on the full Selmer group is nonzero, and Theorem 5.2.3 then identifies the strict kernel with the line generated by \(\kappa_p(E)\).

## Formal logarithm proof

The Kummer maps from
\[
0\longrightarrow E[p^n]\longrightarrow E(\overline{\mathbf Q})
\xrightarrow{p^n} E(\overline{\mathbf Q})\longrightarrow0
\]
are compatible in \(n\). Passing to the inverse limit and tensoring with \(\mathbf Q_p\) gives the global map
\[
E(\mathbf Q)\otimes\mathbf Q_p\longrightarrow H^1(\mathbf Q,V).
\]
Its image is in the Bloch–Kato Selmer group: at every finite place the point's Kummer class is in the finite local condition, and the archimedean condition is automatic. In particular this is the rational-point subspace of \(\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbf Q,V)\).

At \(p\), good reduction identifies the local finite condition with the image of the local Kummer map
\[
E(\mathbf Q_p)\otimes\mathbf Q_p\longrightarrow H^1_f(\mathbf Q_p,V).
\]
The map is injective after tensoring: its integral Kummer kernel consists of torsion, which is killed by tensoring with \(\mathbf Q_p\). The formal-group logarithm on a sufficiently small open subgroup of \(E_1(\mathbf Q_p)\) is an analytic group isomorphism onto an additive lattice in \(\mathbf Q_p\); on \(E(\mathbf Q_p)\) its extension has torsion kernel. Thus
\[
\log_{E,p}(P)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\text{ is torsion in }E(\mathbf Q_p).
\]
The embedding \(E(\mathbf Q)\hookrightarrow E(\mathbf Q_p)\) preserves the order of a rational point: if its image were torsion, the same multiplication relation would hold in \(E(\mathbf Q)\). Hence an infinite-order \(P\) has \(\log_{E,p}(P)\ne0\). Functoriality of the Bloch–Kato logarithm on the Kummer class identifies its logarithmic image with this formal logarithm (up to the chosen nonzero period normalization). Therefore the local Kummer class is nonzero in \(H^1_f(\mathbf Q_p,V)\).

This proof works for a fixed good-reduction prime; ordinarity is needed for the stated Castella theorem, not for the formal-logarithm nonvanishing itself. The claim is about the finite cohomology group, not the singular quotient.

## Consequence under revised Castella §5.2

Castella's revised hypotheses include absolute irreducibility, a prime \(q\parallel N_E\) with the residual ramification condition when \(q\equiv\pm1\pmod p\), and either \(p>5\) or the stated residual image condition. Choice 5.2.1 additionally fixes the inert/split auxiliary data and the two nonvanishing twist values. Theorem 5.2.3 says that when the analytic order is positive and even and the Selmer dimension is two, \(\kappa_p(E)\ne0\) if and only if the localization map is nonzero; in that case it spans \(\ker(\operatorname{loc}_p)\). The point above supplies the nonzero map, so it supplies exactly the missing premise for this theorem. It neither supplies the dimension-two premise nor proves the theorem's auxiliary hypotheses.

At analytic order exactly two, this proves a nonzero strict Selmer class conditional on those premises. It does not prove that \(\kappa_p(E)\) comes from a rational point. The rational Kummer subspace is contained in the full Selmer group, while the quotient is
\[
\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbf Q,V)/(E(\mathbf Q)\otimes\mathbf Q_p)
\simeq T_p\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)\otimes\mathbf Q_p.
\]
The exact sequence does not identify the strict kernel with the rational Kummer subspace.

## One point, Selmer dimension two, and the divisible-Sha alternative

Write \(r=\operatorname{rank}E(\mathbf Q)\), and let
\(t_p=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbf Z_p}\Sha(E/\mathbf Q)[p^\infty]_{\rm div}\).
The standard Selmer exact sequence gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbf Q_p}\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbf Q,V)=r+t_p.
\]
Consequently, dimension two plus one explicit non-torsion rational point leaves exactly these arithmetic possibilities:

* \(r=1,\ t_p=1\): one rational direction and one divisible-Sha direction;
* \(r=2,\ t_p=0\): both Selmer directions are rational Kummer directions.

This is the exact partial result. To get the second case one needs additional non-parity information, for example an explicit second rational point proved independent modulo torsion (a nonsingular canonical-height Gram matrix is a certificate), a descent proving rank at least two, or a theorem eliminating the divisible p-primary Sha contribution in this setting. A two-descent or an explicit point is independent arithmetic input; analytic order two alone does not construct it. No universal method producing that second point for every analytic-rank-two curve is known from the cited hypotheses. The assertion that it always exists is the still-unproved lower-bound direction of weak BSD.

Cassels–Tate parity must be applied to the right object. For an elliptic curve, the Cassels–Tate pairing is alternating and nondegenerate on the reduced quotient \(\Sha/\Sha_{\rm div}\); its finite p-primary part therefore has square order (and the appropriate finite-level paired quotients have even dimension). This says nothing that kills the divisible subgroup: it is precisely the radical removed before obtaining the nondegenerate pairing. In particular, it does **not** exclude \(t_p=1\) in the rank-one alternative above.

There is also a global parity theorem for the p-primary Selmer corank: it has parity equal to the analytic order/root-number parity. For analytic order two, total Selmer corank two is even, exactly as already imposed here. It is not a theorem that the Mordell–Weil rank itself is even absent control of the divisible-Sha contribution. The relation is \(r+t_p\equiv2\pmod2\), allowing \((r,t_p)=(1,1)\) as well as \((2,0)\). Inferring rank parity from Selmer parity by silently setting \(t_p=0\) would assume away the gap.

## Primary references and limits

* F. Castella, [revised author manuscript, *Nonvanishing of Generalised Kato Classes and Iwasawa Main Conjectures*](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/GKC-IMC.pdf), §5.1, Proposition 5.1.2, Corollary 5.1.3, Remark 5.1.4, hypotheses (h1)–(h3), Choice 5.2.1 and Theorem 5.2.3. In particular, the theorem explicitly says the generalized class spans the strict Selmer group when the localization map is nonzero.
* [Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, Appendix C.17](https://www.math.pdmi.ras.ru/~lowdimma/BSD/Silverman-Arithmetic_of_EC.pdf), for the Kummer sequence and Cassels–Tate duality; see also the standard formal-group logarithm on a sufficiently small subgroup. The formal-log argument above is the local Lie-group/Kummer deduction, not a claim of Castella.
* T. Dokchitser and V. Dokchitser, [*On the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer quotients modulo squares*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610290), for the p-parity theorem over \(\mathbf Q\); and [Trihan–Wüthrich, *Parity conjectures for elliptic curves over global fields of positive characteristic*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X1100532X), §6, for the divisible radical discussion. The parity theorem concerns total Selmer corank, not a direct identification with Mordell–Weil rank.

No curve-prime instance is claimed or needed. The conclusion is conditional on Castella's hypotheses and on the two-dimensional Selmer premise; it does not establish either premise uniformly, finiteness of Sha, or weak BSD.
