# Independent review: auxiliary variation and exterior products

## Verdict

The exterior-product obstruction is valid after putting the classes into one common coefficient field, but the existing `auxiliary-lines.md` states its ambient spaces too quickly. Castella–Hsieh construct the classes over a coefficient field (L) (and state the Selmer space in the proof of Theorem A as (S=\operatorname{Sel}(K,V_pE\otimes_{\mathbf Q_p}L))). Consequently, classes attached to different auxiliary data cannot be exterior-multiplied over \(\mathbf Q_p\) merely because their underlying elliptic curve and prime agree. For any finite pair of choices, take a common finite extension (M/\mathbf Q_p) containing their coefficient fields, extend scalars in the fixed Selmer group, and compare there. A varying/infinite family can be compared pairwise this way; no single finite coefficient field for the whole family is asserted.

Precisely, fix (E,p) and (S_0=\operatorname{Sel}(\mathbf Q,V_pE)\) over \(\mathbf Q_p\), with dimension two and localization of rank one. For each admissible datum (a), the paper's projected class is in the corresponding (L_a)-scalar extension of this cohomology, and its Selmer membership is part of the theorem's hypotheses/conclusion. For a pair (a,b), choose (M\supseteq L_a,L_b); the classes lie in (S_0\otimes_{\mathbf Q_p}M). The extended localization still has rank one, so its kernel has dimension one over (M). If both classes are in that kernel, their wedge is zero in \(\bigwedge^2_M(S_0\otimes M)\). This is the sound common-target formulation. It does not establish that the classes descend to (S_0), nor does it establish a single \(\mathbf Q_p\)-valued proportionality scalar for classes defined over different coefficient fields.

## Primary-source checks

Primary source: Castella–Hsieh, *On the non-vanishing of generalized Kato classes for elliptic curves of rank 2*, Forum of Mathematics, Sigma 10 (2022), e12, [published article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/on-the-nonvanishing-of-generalised-kato-classes-for-elliptic-curves-of-rank-2/72ED842AB428A7ED619B1B84287A6864) and [authors' PDF](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/Kato.pdf).

- **Hypotheses and quantifiers.** Theorem A fixes (E,p,K,\psi) and imposes its conditions on that choice: good ordinary (p>3), root number (+1), (L(E,1)=0), nonzero auxiliary twist values, residual irreducibility, (N_f^-) the squarefree product of an odd number of primes, and residual ramification at every prime dividing (N_f^-), together with the prescribed ray-class character/conductor setup. These conditions must be rechecked for each auxiliary datum. The task's two-dimensional Selmer and rank-one localization assumptions are additional fixed-((E,p)) assumptions. The theorem does not quantify over arbitrary (K,\psi) without these conditions.
- **Coefficient field and target.** In §1.2, the four classes are projected from the triple-product cohomology to (H^1(\mathbf Q,V_pE)) (equation (1.8)); the construction and theorem use a finite coefficient field (L), and the proof explicitly tensors (V_pE) and the Selmer space with (L) (§5). The underlying (V_pE) factor is fixed as (K,\psi) vary, but the coefficient field need not be. Thus the comparison has a canonical common target only after scalar extension to a common coefficient field. The paper's representation decomposition (1.7) is into induced constituents; describing the projection as the scalar/isotypic projection is a useful representation-theoretic interpretation, but the source does not normalize it as an End-trace map. The review therefore relies on the natural projection of (1.8)/(1.7), not on an extra trace-normalization claim.
- **Local condition and kernel.** The paper defines the strict Selmer group as \(\ker(\log_p:S\to\mathbf Q_p)\), where \(\log_p\) is restriction followed by the formal-group logarithm (Introduction §1.3). In the proof, the relevant local map is the formal logarithm on the finite local condition (§5.3, Theorem 5.3); note that equation (5.6) is the Selmer eigenspace dimension statement, not the leading-term formula. Theorem A's converse condition is nonzero localization on the Selmer group; in the fixed setting here, the rank-one localization and the good-ordinary Bloch–Kato finite local condition identify the kernel of the formal logarithm with the kernel of localization after scalar extension. This identification applies to Selmer classes, not arbitrary local cohomology classes. It should be stated as an extension of the fixed local map, not as a newly defined map depending on each (K,\psi).
- **Exterior product.** The leading-term formula (5.5) pairs the diagonal class with every (x\in S) as a nonzero scalar times \(\log_{\omega_E,p}(x)\). The paper's rank-two argument uses nondegeneracy/alternation of the relevant derived height to place the diagonal class in the strict kernel; Remark 1.6 gives proportionality of the two diagonal classes for fixed data; this does not furnish a cross-K comparison. Once the common fixed kernel is one-dimensional, the pairwise wedge vanishes by linear algebra, including for classes from different data after common scalar extension. No equality of normalized classes across (K)'s follows.
- **What is and is not uniform.** No verified edition containing the cited Corollary 5.1 has been identified. The assertion formerly attributed to it (dependence modulo \(\mathbf Q^\times\) only on the ring-class character for fixed K, together with a point formula) is withdrawn and must not be used. The verified authors’ PDF and published JEMS article instead have §5.1 and Lemma 5.1; the PDF’s Remark 1.6 and Theorem 5.5 concern the two diagonal classes for fixed data and a point formula under its arithmetic hypotheses. Those are not a Corollary 5.1 and do not establish comparison across distinct quadratic fields. The common-kernel argument does suffice for proportionality over the pairwise common coefficient field, conditional on Selmer membership and the local kernel conclusion for each datum.
- **Other global classes.** The complementary induced constituents in (1.7) have different Galois representations, so they are not additional vectors in the fixed (V_pE)-Selmer space. The twist nonvanishing conditions and the paper's Selmer arguments control those constituents; they do not provide a second class in the fixed target. Mixed-root vanishing is Corollary 4.8 (and Theorem A), under the individual data's hypotheses. Theorem 5.5 / the point-valued formula in the later proof requires the finite (p)-primary Sha condition where it is used; an abstract Selmer class alone does not give a rational-point class.

## Corrections to `auxiliary-lines.md`

1. Replace unconditional \(\mathbf Q_p\)-valued assertions about the constructed classes by statements over each coefficient field (L_a), then compare a finite pair after scalar extension to a common (M). In particular, write the exterior product in \(\bigwedge^2_M(S_0\otimes M)\), not automatically in \(\bigwedge^2_{\mathbf Q_p}S_0\).
2. Lemma 5.1 does prove that each of the four projections lands in the elliptic-curve Selmer group, using complementary Selmer-group vanishing. No verified edition with the purported Corollary 5.1 was found; the unsupported attribution and fixed-K character-dependence assertion are withdrawn above. Do not restore them without a matching citable edition.
3. Do not state that source theorem hypotheses are verified by the rank/localization assumptions. Those are separate assumptions, to be checked for every ((K,\psi)); the published Theorem A explicitly includes root number (+1), (L(E,1)=0), and the odd-squarefree (N_f^-) condition in addition to the twist and residual hypotheses.
4. Qualify the “trace projection” and the displayed CM decomposition as interpretations requiring choices/identifications; the primary source formulates the natural projection through (1.7). They are not needed for the shared-kernel argument.

## Remaining mathematical gap

This proves only dependence of the specified projected classes in the fixed strict line, under the stated hypotheses. It neither proves the existence of admissible auxiliary data for every (E,p), nor yields rational points or a rational-rank lower bound. A proposed second global class would need its own construction into the same (V_pE)-Selmer group, its local condition checked, and its coefficient field aligned as above; no such class follows from the complementary constituents or auxiliary variation audited here.
